The Distance Between "Hello" and "Goodbye"

On how we love each other wrong, lose people along the way, and keep walking into the same fire hoping this time it won't burn.

— Sam Waita  ·  March 12, 2026

Guest write and a friend of mwangi's  ·  Originally published on Sam's Substack

Yeah, I believe it's high time we admit that love in uni is lowkey a scam, rather, that's what I would have said if I had zero cognitive capability. Not because young individuals don't love enough. Not because we're heartless and commitment-phobic. But because we've built an entire culture around loving each other wrong, and no one has taught us any different.

It starts the same way every time. The spark. The late-night calls. The feeling that this one is different. You meet someone who makes your chest tight and mind quiet, and suddenly campus feels less like survival and more like somewhere you actually wanna be. You make plans, you make promises, you make each other believe that this time the usual rules don't apply.

Then the first crack appears 😭. A text left on read, a tone misread, a test neither of you knew you were taking. And instead of a conversation, there's silence. Instead of "This hurt me," there's "If he wanted to he would." Instead of vulnerability there's performance. You wait for the other person to prove that they care instead of telling them you need to feel it. And somewhere in that silence the cycle is born. From there it's a script neither of you wrote, but both of you follow. A fight breaks out, words are thrown — really cruel ones at times. Space is taken, reconnection happens, but nothing actually changes. Repeat. Over and over until the love is still there, but the trust has been buried so deep you can't find it anymore.

And then comes the isolation. Slowly at first. You cancel on friends to wait for a call that may not come. You stop explaining the fights because you are tired of hearing "just leave" from people who don't understand. Then one day someone you've known for years sits you down and tells you "Man, I don't even recognise you anymore." Or worse, they just stop calling. And you realise you've been so busy trying to hold onto one person that you've let everyone else slip away.

"You watch a friendship die not because it needed to, but because someone needed proof."

The hardest part is when the choice becomes explicit. When your partner looks at a friendship you've had for years, someone who was there before they arrived, someone who knows your history, someone who has never been anything but a friend, and says "It's me or them." No negotiation, no trust. Just an ultimatum wrapped in insecurity. And because you are young and in love and so terrified of losing the person that makes your chest tight, you choose. You watch a friendship die not because it needed to, but because someone needed proof. And the friend walks away confused, wondering what they did wrong, wondering if the years meant nothing. They become collateral damage to a war they didn't even sign up for.

And you let them. Because love, you tell yourself, requires sacrifice. But no one tells you some sacrifices leave you hollow.

Then there's social media. I know it usually gets a shout in conversations like this but people rarely touch on how deep its roots venture into the human psyche. Once social media is involved, privacy is thrown out of the window the performance is now in session. The subtweets. The WhatsApp statuses designed to cut without naming. Turning pain into content and calling it catharsis. But all it really does is make sure the person we love knows they've hurt us — without having to hear our voice say it. Trading intimacy for audience and wondering why we feel so alone.

"We drag people from the past into present fights and wonder why the present can't breathe."

And then there are the ghosts. The exes who haunt every argument without ever being in the room. The comparisons that start as questions and end as weapons. "You probably loved her more." "He never made me feel like this." We measure love by metrics that don't even make sense — effort without definition, presence without quality, words without follow-through.

The saddest part? It was never about grand gestures. No one was asking for cars or public declarations. It was about checking in, showing up, asking before being asked. The small things that become the whole war. And when those small things didn't come, the begging starts. The paragraphs. The tears. The "do you even see me?" that was really just asking to be held.

And one day someone wakes up and realises they've spent months losing themselves in someone else. They realise they've been carrying hope like a wound, waiting for change that never comes. They realise loving someone has cost them the ability to love themselves. So they leave. Not because they don't love anymore. Because they finally love themselves more. And sometimes they look around and realise the people they lost along the way aren't coming back either. Love took them too.

That's not a villain story. That's not blame. That's just two people who loved each other and couldn't figure out how to make that love land without destroying everything else in the process.

"CIRCUMSTANCE explains it. It does not EXCUSE it!!!! And the scars, no matter how explainable, still stay."

Maybe that's the unfortunate truth about romance in uni, but in general. Not that it fails. But that it fails in the same way, over and over, with the same patterns, the same silences, the same performances, the same ultimatums. And we keep walking into it hoping this time will be different, without ever asking ourselves what we're actually bringing to the table besides hope. Without asking who we're becoming in the name of love. Without asking whose bodies we're stepping over to keep it alive.

Because hope, it turns out, isn't enough. It never was.
