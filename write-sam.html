THE DISTANCE BETWEEN "HELLO" AND "GOODBYE"

The unfortunate truth

— Sam Waita  ·  Mar 12, 2026

Guest write and a friend of mwangi's  ·  Originally published on Sam's Substack

Yeah, I believe its high time we admit that 'love' in uni is lowkey a scam, rather, that's what I would have said if I had zero cognitive capability. Not because young individuals don't love enough. Not because we're heartless and commitment-phobic. But because we've built an entire culture around loving each other wrong, and no one has taught us any different.

It starts the same way every time. The spark. The late-night calls. The feeling that this one is different. You meet someone who makes your chest tight and mind quiet, and suddenly campus feels less like survival and more like somewhere you actually wanna be. Depending on whether or not you're both in the same uni. You make plans, you make promises, you make each other believe that this time the usual rules don't apply.

Then the first crack appears😭. A text left on read, a tone misread, a test neither of you knew you were taking. And instead of a fucking conversation, there's silence. Instead of "This hurt me," there's "If he wanted to he would." Instead of vulnerability there's performance. You wait for the other person to prove that they care instead of telling them you need to feel it. And somewhere in that silence the cycle is born. From there, its a script neither of you wrote, but both of you follow. A fight breaks out, word are thrown, really cruel ones at times. Space is taken, reconnection happens, but nothing actually changes. REPEAT. Over and over until the love is still there, but the trust has been buried so deep you can't find it no more.

And then comes the isolation. Slowly at first. You cancel on friends to wait for a call that may not come. You stop explaining the fights because you are tired of hearing "just leave" from people who don't understand. Then one day someone you've known for years sits you down and tells you "Man, I don't even recognize you anymore." Or worse, they just stop calling. And you realize you've been so busy trying to hold onto one person that you've let everyone else slip away.

The hardest part is when the choice becomes explicit. When your partner looks at a friendship you've had for years, someone who was there before they arrived, someone who knows your history, someone who has never been nothing but a friend, and says "Its me or them." No negotiation, no trust. Just an ultimatum wrapped in insecurity. And because you are young and 'in love' and so terrified of losing the person that makes your makes your chest tight, you choose. You choose them. You watch a friendship die not because it needed to, but because someone needed proof. And the friend? They walk away confused, wondering what they did wrong, wondering if the years meant null. They become collateral damage to a war they didn't even sign up for. And you let them. Because 'love', you tell yourself, requires sacrifice. But no one tells you some sacrifices leave you hollow. As Oscar Wilde once said, "The tragedy of love isn't death or separation. the tragedy is that we stop loving someone while they are still here, still waiting, still giving." You watch yourself become someone you don't recognize, and the worst part is that you keep choosing it.

The ones who were there before. The ones who would've stayed no matter what. The ones you chose against every time the ultimatum came. And you chose 'love'. Every time.…. Until 'love' is all you are left with and its still not enough. Until you look around and the people who knew you before are gone, and the person you sacrificed them for is still holding the door open, waiting for the next thing to prove your loyalty.

Ooooh SOCIAL MEDIA. I know it usually gets a shout in such conversations but people rarely touch on how its roots ventures deep in the human psyche. Once social media is involved, privacy is thrown out of the window, the performance is now in sesh. Tiktok reposts. The subtweets, for those who use twitter(I'll never conform and call it X). The whatsapp statuses designed to cut without naming. Turning pain to content and calling it catharsis. But all it really does is make sure the person we 'love' knows they've hurt us. All without having to hear our voice say it. Trading intimacy for audience and wondering why we feel so alone.

And then there are the ghosts. The exes who haunt every argument without ever being in the room. The comparisons that start as questions and end as weapons. "You probs loved her more." "He never made me feel like this." We drag people from the past into present fights and wonder why the present can't breathe. We measure 'love' by metrics that don't even make sense, effort without definition, presence without quality, words without follow-through.

The saddest part? Its never about grand gestures. No one was asking for cars or tips or public declarations. It was about checking in, showing up, asking before being asked. The small things that become the whole war. And when those small things didn't come, the begging starts. The paragraphs. The tears. The "do you even see me?" that was really just asking to be held.

And one day someone wakes up and realizes they've spent months losing themselves in someone else. They realize they've been carrying hope like a wound, waiting for change that never comes. They realize 'loving' someone has cost them the ability to love themselves. So they leave. Not because they don't 'love' anymore. Because they finally love themselves more. And sometimes they look around and realize the people they lost along the way aren't coming back either. 'Love' took them too.

That's not a villain story. That's not blame. That's just two people who 'loved' each other and couldn't figure out how to make that 'love' land without destroying everything else in the process. The friend who got cut off? They're still out there carrying their own quiet hurt, wondering why they weren't worth fighting for. And maybe one day they'll understand it wasn't about them. Maybe they won't. Maybe some bridges just burn.

CIRCUMSTANCE explains it, it doesn't EXCUSE it!!! And the scars no matter how explainable, still stay.

Maybe that's the unfortunate truth about romance in uni, you know what?? Scratch that. Romance in general. Not that it fails. But that it fails in the same way, over and over, with the same patterns, the same silences, the same performances, the same ultimatums. And we keep walking into it hoping this time will be different, without ever asking ourselves what we're actually bringing on the table besides hope. Without asking who we're becoming in the name of 'love'. Without asking who's bodies we're stepping over to keep it alive.

Because HOPE, it turns out, isn't enough, It never was.
