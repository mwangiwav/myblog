The Performance Paradox

You, Edited

— mwangi  ·  23rd June 2026

There is a version of you that only exists when other people are watching. You know them. They sit up a little straighter. They choose their words more carefully. They laugh at the right moments and goes quiet at the right ones too. They are not fake exactly but they are not fully you either. They are you, edited.

I used to think this was a social media problem. That social media specifically had done something to people, turned them into highlight reels of themselves, curated and filtered and captioned into something that barely resembles a human life. But the more I sat with it the more I realised social media did not create the performance. It just handed it a stage.

Think about the last time you were completely alone. Not alone with your phone nearby, not alone in the way where you're still mentally narrating your day to someone. Actually alone, with no audience real or imagined. How long did it take before you stopped performing?

Because I think most of us are performing even then. Performing for a future version of the story. Performing for the person we wish was watching. Performing for ourselves, which might be the strangest performance of all.

"The desire to be witnessed is not a flaw in our character. It is the character. It has always been."

Humans have always needed an audience. Before Instagram there were diaries written in the hope someone would one day read them. Before selfies there were portraits commissioned by people who wanted to be remembered in a specific way. Before personal branding there was reputation which is just personal branding with slower distribution. The need did not change. Only the speed did.

So here is where it gets uncomfortable. If performance is just human nature, if we have always been doing this then what exactly are we chasing when we talk about being authentic?

I scroll through Instagram and I see people who have built entire identities around not performing. The "I don't care what you think" post that clearly took three attempts to get right. The "raw and unfiltered" caption on a photo that is very much filtered. The "just existing" story that was very much considered before posting. There is nothing wrong with any of it but it is still a performance. It is the performance of authenticity. And somehow that one is the most seductive because it asks you to believe it is not a performance at all.

"Authenticity became an aesthetic before most of us noticed it happening."

The paradox is this: the moment you decide to "be yourself" online, you have already made a decision. You have chosen which self. You have chosen what lighting. You have chosen what that self is going to say and look how and when. That act of choosing however honest it feels is curation and curation is performance. There is no way out of it.

I am not saying this to be cynical. I am saying it because I think accepting it is actually more honest than the alternative. The alternative is pretending you have transcended the very human need to be seen and validated which is itself a performance, and a particularly exhausting one.

Maybe the more interesting question is not "how do I stop performing" but "which version of me do I actually want to put on stage" because you are going to perform either way. You might as well perform something close to true.

The real you is probably somewhere in the gap between what you post and what you never would. Not the highlight and not the void somewhere in the middle, watching both, deciding what to do with them. That person is the most interesting one in the room. It's just that nobody has figured out how to post them yet.
